# Bronze/Iron to Classical transition

This tranche extends Sim-World without introducing a conventional science-point tech tree. New practices emerge from accumulated craft experience, material opportunity, military/economic pressure and contact with societies that already use them.

## New breakthroughs

- Light chariotry: horse husbandry + war horses + craft/smithing + military pressure.
- Mounted cavalry: sustained horse husbandry and war-horse experience; chariot experience helps but is not mandatory.
- Hydraulic engineering: requires water management and practical infrastructure experience; supports aqueduct-scale works.
- Standard weights and measures: grows out of markets, traders and administration.
- Coinage: requires standard measures, silver and organised markets/administration.
- Mass heavy infantry: iron-working + dense populations + mobilisation pressure.
- Military drill: repeated organisation of substantial armies, especially mass infantry.
- Relay administration: roads + administration + transport horses.

## Urbanisation

Regions now have an urban population and an urban carrying capacity. Urbanisation changes gradually rather than appearing instantly after construction. The current limiting factors are water, food/storage, urban employment, and settlement scale. Aqueducts can therefore enable much larger cities without directly creating population.

## Environmental hazards and hidden externalities

Hazards are simulated separately from what inhabitants understand about them. Each hazard has:

- source strength
- current exposure
- accumulated burden
- recognition state
- mitigation level

This is intended as a general framework for lead, asbestos, fossil-fuel air pollution, CFCs and later industrial hazards.

Lead is deliberately modelled as useful rather than as a trap chosen by an omniscient player. Lead-bearing water infrastructure can cheaply expand urban water capacity. Exposure then accumulates gradually and produces chronic health/productivity effects. Those effects should exist whether or not the cause is recognised. UI and policy systems should only expose the causal attribution once a society develops enough observational/medical/statistical capability to recognise it.

Lead should not be treated as a single-cause explanation for Roman decline. Its simulated effects are material but deliberately too small to topple a healthy empire on their own.
