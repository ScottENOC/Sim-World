# Early-modern military transition v1

This package extends existing gunpowder, steel, siege, fleet and non-state systems rather than creating parallel military models.

## Firearms and steel

Early firearms remain possible with bronze and iron. Steelmaking is not a prerequisite. Where steel industry develops and military steel adoption spreads, firearm combat quality improves through better barrels, locks, fittings and reliability.

## Gunpowder artillery

Gunpowder societies gradually build bombards and field cannon. Guns are persistent inventory items with recorded construction metal (iron, bronze or steel). Campaigns physically take artillery from the home inventory and return surviving pieces afterwards. Artillery consumes gunpowder and shot while engaged, is constrained by campaign supply, reduces fortification advantage and contributes modest field combat power.

## Naval gunnery

Existing persistent ships remain authoritative. Coastal gunpowder societies with working harbours and dockyards gradually manufacture naval guns, which are allocated across ships during battle according to hull capacity. Advanced warships can carry much heavier gun batteries than basic war boats. Naval guns consume powder and shot in combat; steel improves gun quality but is not required.

## Irregular technology diffusion

Bandits do not run their own research system. Where firearms and powder are locally available in unstable or unsafe regions, they can leak or be stolen into bandit hands. Armed bandits become harder to suppress.

Pirate havens and mercenary organisations similarly learn portable military practices from host regions over time. Pirate suppression therefore compares state strength against pirate military capacity adjusted for diffused firearm, naval-gunnery and steel capability.

The intended progression is technology diffusion through contact, theft, employment and local availability rather than free era-based upgrades.
