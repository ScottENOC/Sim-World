# Map geography principles

The permanent land map represents durable physical/geographic areas. It does **not** encode modern sovereignty or guarantee the existence of historical states.

## Region test

Create a standalone permanent region when physical geography makes the area materially distinct at game scale. Relevant reasons include:

- island or island group separation;
- peninsula or isthmus isolation;
- mountain basin or strongly separated valley;
- major river/delta geography;
- strategically important strait or maritime chokepoint;
- a coastal pocket whose movement/trade/defence would be meaningfully lost if merged into its hinterland.

Small political jurisdictions are **not** standalone regions merely because they are sovereign today.

## Microstates and enclaves

Default: absorb a modern microstate/enclave into the surrounding geographic region.

- Vatican City -> surrounding Rome/Lazio geography. A future holy city-state must emerge through settlements, religion and politics.
- San Marino -> surrounding Italian geography.
- Monaco -> surrounding French coastal geography.
- Liechtenstein -> surrounding Swiss/Rhine-Alpine geography.

Exceptions are geography-driven, not sovereignty-driven:

- Gibraltar remains a standalone region because the Rock is a strongly isolated fortified peninsula controlling the Strait of Gibraltar.
- Andorra/Pyrenean valleys may remain standalone because the high mountain basin can be geographically distinct at this map scale.
- Islands and comparable chokepoints may likewise remain separate when the physical geography warrants it.

The same rule applies to historical city-states. Athens, Rome, Venice, a holy city or an emergent trading city need not have a permanent tiny land polygon. A settlement can become politically independent inside a larger geographic region.

## Source borders

Modern administrative boundaries are geometry ingredients only. Builders should merge, clip or absorb them into durable game-geographic regions. Existing simulated geography wins when source datasets disagree, and later expansions must subtract already simulated land before adding new regions.

## Expansion boundary

The second broad expansion completes geographic Europe through Ukraine, Belarus and European Russia (clipped near the Urals), plus the modern Middle East and Caucasus. Siberia, Central Asia, India and the rest of the world remain later tranches so testing does not immediately jump to a full-world region count.
